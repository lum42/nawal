# Cosmovisión maya — los veinte días, across traditions

Working paper for the `maya-cosmovision` project. What the documented sources actually say
about the twenty day-signs, and where they disagree with each other. Companion artefact:
`index.html` (interactive wheel, table, bridges, date calculator, sources).

Compiled 2026-11-24. All claims below carry a source; anything derived is labelled **derived**.

---

## 0. The method, and the one rule

Three evidence tiers are in play, and they are not interchangeable:

| Tier | What | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| A | peer-reviewed / primary (Weeks, Sachse & Prager 2009, Spero 1991, Stuart's *Day Sign Notes*) | glyph readings, divinatory character, etymologies |
| B | institutional / organised tradition (Smithsonian NMAI + Komon Tohil of Zunil; Guatemala's Ministerio de Cultura) | modern highland meanings, cross-language name tables |
| C | community-facing / popular (atitlanvida, IMAP, nomada.gt) | orientation only — never a sole basis for a claim |

**The rule:** the twenty *positions* in the count are fixed and shared. The *names* are not
translations of each other. Every table that presents "the Maya day sign X = meaning Y" is
collapsing at least four different reading traditions into one, and usually the wrong one.

---

## 1. The skeleton

- 20 named days × 13 numbers = 260 days. The 20 and the 13 are coprime, so every
  combination occurs exactly once per 260.
- Names are stable across languages **by position**: same slot, different word.
- The count is attested from c. 500 BC (Monte Albán, San José Mogote) and in Maya territory
  from c. 200 BC (San Bartolo, Kaminaljuyú) — Ministerio de Cultura, *Armonización Maya*.
- Highland names for the calendar itself: K'iche' *Aj Ilabal Q'ij* ("the sense of the day");
  Kaqchikel *Cholq'ij* ("the organization of time"). *Tzolk'in* is a Yucatec coinage.
- Numbers carry valence (Bunzel 1952, via Weeks, Sachse & Prager 2009): 1–3 "gentle" (thanks, favours),
  7–9 "indifferent" (commemoration), 11–13 "violent" (defence, vengeance, sorcery).

## 4. Layer 3 — the living K'iche' meanings

Komon Tohil, the K'iche' daykeepers of Zunil, published the etymologies the Smithsonian
distributes. This is the layer most websites are actually quoting, and it is a *practice*
document, not a philological one. Full text is in `days.json` under `kt`.

Representative entries:

- **Imox** — sea, river, lake. Ask for rain; calm mental and spiritual disorder.
- **Iq'** — wind, air, spirit, "heart of the sky"; also heavy showers and hurricanes.
- **K'at** — net, tangle, disentangle; symbol of fire, burning, the fishing net.
- **Kej** — deer; one of the four supports of sky and earth; the four corners.
- **Q'anil** — seed and yellow; the colour of gold and cane; the day of the milpa.
- **Toj** — offering, fine, payment; **the nawal of fire**.
- **B'atz'** — thread; the day new daykeepers are initiated and the 260-day year turns.
- **I'x** — jaguar; "the sacred name of divinity on earth", nawal of domestic animals.
- **Kawoq** — thunder, ants, woman; predictors of the future and judges.
- **Ajpu'** — life, destiny, plants, animals; the Lord Sun; hunter and marksman.

## 5. Layer 4 — divinatory character (the sourced one)

Weeks (2006) synthesises Bunzel 1952 (Chichicastenango), Schultze Jena 1946, Tedlock 1992 and
Edmonson 1997 into a day-by-day character, split into **good days** (commemoration) and **bad
days** (defence). This is the most defensible "meaning" layer in English, because four
ethnographers converge.

- **Bad (8):** Imox, Iq', Aq'ab'al, K'at, Kan, Kame, Toj, Tz'i'
- **Good (10):** Kej, Q'anil, E, Aj, I'x, Tz'ikin, Ajmaq, No'j, Tijax, B'atz'* (*bad in 1854, good for ancestors)
- **Indifferent (2):** Kawoq, Ajpu'

That asymmetry is itself a finding: the K'iche' frame is heavy on days that require defensive
ritual, and treats the solar end of the count (Kawoq, Ajpu') as neutral.

---

## 5b. The office column — editorial synthesis, not a sourced category

The `office` field in the dataset (Aj = the staff, Tijax = healer-surgeon, Kawoq = the
collective, and so on) is **this project's synthesis** across the sources below. No single
source tabulates it; Estrada Peña 2015 and Cano et al. 2020 discuss social roles for some signs
only. It is labelled as synthesis in the app, and 'midwife' is not attested in these sources and is not used.

## 6. Layer 5 — the animal companion

A second register, not a correction of the glyph layer:

- Serpent (Kan) · deer (Kej) · dog (Tz'i') · monkey (B'atz') · jaguar (I'x) · bird/eagle (Tz'ikin)
  · owl (Kame as herald of death; Ajmaq in some readings) · **turtle (Kawoq)**.
- The rest are forces, objects or states. The count is a taxonomy of powers, not a bestiary —
  absence of an animal is not a gap.
- Kawoq = turtle is attested independently: Guatemala's embassy nawal pages, mayan-calendar.com
  (compiled from Tedlock/Stanzione), Kenneth Johnson's *Nawales*, and Estrada Peña 2015 in
  *Estudios de Cultura Maya*.

## 7. Layer 6 — direction and colour

Documented, but it belongs to **directions and year-bearers**, not to each of the twenty days:

- East red · North white · West black · South yellow (atitlanvida, citing Maya Archaeologist;
  the four-direction scheme is standard in the wider Maya corpus).
- The Dresden new-year pages colour the **year-bearers**: Iq' white, Kej black, E yellow,
  No'j red (Ministerio de Cultura, *Armonización Maya*, reading the Códice de Dresden pages).
- **The ±5 relation is the year-bearer sets, not a direction group.** The four days that can
  carry a year sit five positions apart — Iq'/Kej/E/No'j (2, 7, 12, 17) in the Classic and
  highland counts, K'at/Toj/Ix/Kawoq (4, 9, 14, 19) in the Yucatec *juego K'at/Kan*
  (Armonización Maya, Cuadro 4a). The **directions** are four *consecutive* blocks of five
  days, one per quarter — the two schemes are different and must not be conflated.
- The *Cruz Maya* of highland practice is a nine-position figure: engendramiento, nacimiento and
  destino, each with a left and right arm (nomada.gt, Julio David Menchú). The arms' offsets are
  **not** standardised across sources — do not invent them.

## 8. The elements question

**Short answer: none of the sources consulted documents a pre-Hispanic Maya system of four
elements (earth / air / fire / water) attached to the twenty day-signs** — an assessment of the
evidence below, not an argument found in any single source. That mapping is a colonial and modern overlay,
structurally borrowed from European/Aztec-influenced frameworks and popularised in New Age
literature. Any chart that assigns *elemento* to all twenty is doing that overlay, usually
without saying so.

What the sources do give, and what "thunder for Kawak" correctly rests on, is that **individual
days carry a natural force**, often in the word itself:

| Day | Force | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Kawoq / Kawak / Cauac** | **thunder, lightning, storm** | **etymological** — proto-Mayan *kahoq |
| Imox | water — sea, river, lake | Komon Tohil etymology |
| Iq' | wind, air, breath, spirit | Komon Tohil + Classic glyph |
| K'at | fire, the net | Komon Tohil |
| Toj | fire; offering, debt | Komon Tohil ("nawal of fire") |
| I'x | earth; jaguar | Weeks (Bunzel/Tedlock), Komon Tohil |
| Q'anil | maize seed, ripeness | glyph (Ek'/Lamat) + Komon Tohil |
| Kej | earth and sky as four supports | Komon Tohil |
| Tijax | flint, obsidian, the blade | Komon Tohil + glyph |
| Kame | death, ancestors | glyph + Weeks |

So the honest formulation is: **the days are powers, not elements**; a handful of them name a
natural force outright, and Kawak is the strongest case in the entire set because the force is
in the reconstructed root, not in an interpretive gloss.

### Kawak in detail

- Proto-Mayan **\*kahoq** "lightning, thunder" (Kaufman & Norman 1984:117), surviving in 21+
  languages: *kawoq* (K'iche', Ixil), *kahoq* (Kaqchikel, Poqomchi'), *kyooq* (Mam),
  *chahwuk* (Tzeltal), *chauk* (Tzotzil), *chawuk* (Tojolabal, Chuj), *chahk* (Chol),
  *cháwAk* (Chontal), *chaak* (Yucatec, Mopan, Itzá).
- Yucatec keeps *kawak* for the day and uses *chaak* for the rain god — two **doublets** of one
  root, not one word in two grammatical roles. The day is not the god, but the connection is
  real rather than a mistake: the day's own zoomorph (the Cauac Monster) *is* the Earth Lord and
  storm god, and in Tzotzil the day name *chauk* is used for the deity itself. Chaak
  (personage), K'awiil (lightning god, royal sceptre) and Kawak (the day) remain three different
  orders of thing; "Kawak is the rain god" is a category slip, not a fantasy.
- Glyph T528, the "Cauac Monster": grape-cluster and swirl markings framed by water dots.
  Dicey Taylor (1978) showed it marks a **locale** — a cave, a throne, the surface of the earth —
  not Thompson's celestial dragon. It appears as the pedestal under rulers at Bonampak and
  Palenque, and as the frame of cave scenes.
- Spero (1991) reads the Kawak also as ancestor, warrior and patron of witchcraft; at Bonampak
  Stela 1 an ancestral figure (or the Maize God) looks out from the cleft in its forehead.
- Highland practice adds two non-storm senses: **ants** and **woman** (Komon Tohil) — and the
  animal companion is the **turtle**. Those sit alongside the storm, they do not replace it.

## 9. Where the traditions genuinely diverge

These are the findings worth keeping; each is a slot where the traditions are not saying the
same thing.

1. **Slot 4 — K'at vs K'an, a false friend.** Colonial orthography writes slot 4 as *Kan*, but
   the word is **k'an**, "ripe, yellow maize" — not *kan*, "serpent". The serpent is slot 5 in
   every tradition (Yucatec Chikchan, K'iche' Kan). What replaced *k'an at slot 4 in K'iche'an is
   **K'at**, "net, fire". The Smithsonian table pairs them exactly so: K'AT–K'AN, KAN–CHIKCHAN.
   Separately, the *juego K'at/Kan* is not this difference at all — it is the set of four
   **year-bearers** used in Postclassic Yucatan (K'at, Toj, Ix, Kawoq), named for the Yucatec
   spelling of K'at (Armonización Maya, Cuadro 4a).
2. **Slot 8 — rabbit vs seed.** Aztec Tochtli and the Yucatec gloss Lamat give *rabbit*; the
   Classic glyph reads Venus star / ripe maize; K'iche' Q'anil is *seed, yellow, maize*. Three
   domains, one slot.
3. **Slot 17 — earth vs thought.** Yucatec Kab'an = earth; Aztec Ollin = movement; K'iche' No'j =
   wisdom, counsel. The sharpest divergence in the set.
4. **Slot 16 — wax vs will.** Yucatec Kib' = wax; Aztec Cozcacuauhtli = vulture; K'iche' Ajmaq =
   will, the prevention of mistakes.
5. **Slot 13 — reed, with a maize homophone.** Both branches mean reed/cane: Yucatec *b'en*,
   highland *aj*, Nahuatl *acatl*. K'iche' *aj* *also* means young ear of maize — a separate
   homophone (proto-Mayan *\*ajn* 'elote' vs *\*aaj* 'reed') — which is where the modern maize
   reading comes from. The divergence is in the gloss, not the word.
6. **Slot 3 — Woton/Watan.** Chuj and Q'anjob'al keep a root unrelated to Aq'ab'al/Ak'bal.

## 10. Coverage — what is closed and what is not

Kaufman 1989 (*The Day-Names of the Meso-American Calendar: A Linguistic Perspective*, tabulated
in the Wikipedia *Maya calendar* article) supplies a full twenty-name column for **Mam, Ixil,
Poqomchi', Proto-Ch'olan, Tzeltal, Chicomuceltec, Chuj, Jakaltek and Q'anjob'al** — that closes
five of the six coverage gaps in the first draft of this paper. Kanjobal and Chuj in that table agree with the
Ministerio de Cultura's Cuadro 1 apart from minor spellings (Q'anjob'al Kaq vs Kajh).

A separate, later source closes the **glosses** for Ixil and Mam: Cano, Estrada, Page & Zent
(*Estudios de Cultura Maya* LVI, 2020) publish the twenty *Alaxïk* with etymologies from Zapil
2007, ordered from B'atz' (the same ceremonial entry point Komon Tohil uses), synthesising
Kaqchikel, K'iche', Mam and Ixil daykeepers. Useful specifics:

- **Ixil Kawoq** is decomposed *k-* + *-a-* + *-woq* "to make noise when speaking", translated
  *trueno* — an independent, non-etymological-dictionary confirmation of the thunder reading.
- Ixil symbolic readings: Ix = the feminine principle and Mother Earth; Kiej = the four corners
  and the four colours of maize; Tz'i' = intense desire, and juridical/legal petitions;
  Kawoq = the collective, the strong word, and turtles.
- **Tz'utujil, Kaqchikel and Sakapultek** are stated to use the K'iche' system outright, so
  Tz'utujil needs no separate column.

Still open, and not filled here:

- **Q'eqchi'** — no published list of the twenty located in this pass.
- **Lacandón** — the ritual calendar is documented (Bruce, Boremanse) but the day-name list was
  not extractable from the sources reached.
- **Ch'orti'** — Ch'olan, so the Proto-Ch'olan column is the closest documented neighbour; a
  modern published list was not located.

The app shows this as a coverage matrix rather than a silent gap.
